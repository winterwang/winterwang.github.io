## Abstract

**Objectives:** To assess the retention rate, changes in disease activity and patient-reported outcomes (PROs), and predictors of treatment discontinuation among baricitinib-treated patients with rheumatoid arthritis (RA) in routine clinical practice in Japan.

**Methods:** This ongoing, multicentre, prospective, observational study (starting February 2021) included adults aged ≥20 years receiving baricitinib. Retention rate and effectiveness outcomes were evaluated through 12 months. Associations between patient characteristics and retention were examined using Random Forest analysis.

**Results:** This interim analysis included 353 patients (mean [standard deviation; SD] age 63.4 [13.6] years; 70.0% female). The 12-month retention rate was 70.2% (95% confidence interval [CI] 65.1, 74.6). The 12-month remission rates were 35.3%, 41.1%, and 79.6% per Clinical Disease Activity Index, Simplified Disease Activity Index, and Disease Activity Score with 28 joints–C-reactive protein. Mean (SD) changes in PROs from baseline were −33.8 (27.8) for physician global assessment, −18.3 (29.4) for patient global assessment, −0.33 (0.53) for Health Assessment Questionnaire-Disability Index, and −19.4 (27.0) for pain. Ten baseline variables contributed most to retention predictions in the Random Forest model.

**Conclusions:** Evidence from this interim analysis suggested the favourable effectiveness and drug survival of baricitinib in real-world Japanese patients with RA.
